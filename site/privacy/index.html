Privacy policy

For Datebook, a calendar plugin for the Omarchy desktop on Linux.

Last updated 26 September 2026.

The short version

Datebook runs entirely on your own computer. It has no server, no accounts and no analytics. Nobody — including its author — can see your calendar. If you connect Google Calendar, your computer talks to Google directly, and the data it fetches is stored only on that computer.

Who is responsible

Datebook is free, open-source software published by Andre (GitHub @Neaxic), the data controller for the purposes of this policy. Contact: CONTACT_EMAIL.

Data Datebook handles

Events you create yourself

Stored as a plain JSON file at ~/.config/datebook/events.json on your computer. They are never transmitted anywhere.

Google user data, if you connect Google Calendar

Connecting is optional and off by default. Datebook works fully without it. If you do connect it, Datebook accesses:

ScopeDataWhy
.../auth/calendar.events The events on the calendars you choose: their titles, times, dates, descriptions, locations and recurrence. To display them in the bar, the grid and the day agenda, and to create, edit and delete events when you do so in Datebook.
.../auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly The names, identifiers, colours and your access level for the calendars on your account. To show you the list so you can choose which calendars Datebook should sync.

Datebook does not request the broad calendar scope and therefore cannot create, rename or delete a calendar — only the events inside the calendars you selected.

How that data is stored

  • Events fetched from Google are cached at ~/.config/datebook/cache.json on your computer, so the calendar still draws when you are offline.
  • The cache is kept separate from your local events file. A sync cannot overwrite what you wrote locally, and a local edit cannot overwrite Google.
  • Your OAuth tokens are stored at ~/.config/datebook/google-tokens.json with file permissions 0600 — readable only by your user account. They are never logged or displayed.

How that data is transmitted

Datebook makes HTTPS requests from your computer to Google's Calendar API (googleapis.com) and Google's OAuth endpoints, and to nowhere else.

There is no Datebook server. Your calendar data is never sent to the author or to any third party, because there is nothing to send it to. Datebook contains no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no advertising.

Limited Use disclosure

Datebook's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Specifically, Google user data obtained through these scopes is used only to provide and improve the calendar features described above. It is not transferred to anyone else, is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not read by any human — it never leaves your device.

Retention and deletion

You are in control of every copy, because every copy is on your own disk.

  • Remove one calendar: click it on Datebook's CALENDARS page. Its cached events are deleted immediately.
  • Disconnect entirely: press DISCONNECT. Datebook revokes its access token with Google, deletes the stored tokens, and deletes every cached Google event.
  • Revoke from Google's side: remove Datebook at myaccount.google.com/permissions at any time.
  • Remove everything: delete the ~/.config/datebook/ directory and uninstall the plugin.

There is no retention period to state, because the author retains nothing.

Your rights

Datebook is used from Denmark and is available worldwide, so the GDPR may apply to you. Rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction and portability are all exercised directly: the data is in readable JSON files on your own machine, which you can inspect, edit, export or delete without asking anyone. The author holds no copy and so has nothing to disclose, correct or erase on your behalf.

Children

Datebook is not directed at children under 13 and collects nothing from anyone.
